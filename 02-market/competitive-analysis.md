# Competitive Analysis

> This is a strategic comparison, not a claim of exhaustive market coverage.

## Competitive set

| Product type | Strength | Weakness / opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Bank app | Trusted transaction source | Often optimized for banking, not behavior change |
| Spreadsheet | Flexible | Manual and high effort |
| Budgeting app | Strong planning | Can become configuration-heavy |
| Expense tracker | Easy categorization | Often passive |
| Investment app | Strong wealth tools | Not focused on everyday cash-flow control |
| MoneyFlow | Action-oriented financial check-in | Must earn trust and demonstrate accuracy |

## Competitive thesis

MoneyFlow should **not** win by matching every feature.

It should win by making the core decision easier:

> **"What is my financial situation, what changed, and what should I do next?"**

## Differentiation

1. Action over dashboards
2. Explainability over opaque recommendations
3. Privacy over data maximization
4. Flexible planning over rigid budgets
5. Narrow MVP over feature sprawl

## Strategic moat hypothesis

The long-term moat is not the UI. It is the combination of:

**personal context + behavior history + explainable decision support + trust**

This must be validated rather than assumed.
