# Problem Statement

## Problem

Young adults can see their transactions but often cannot quickly answer:

> **"Am I financially on track this month, and what should I do next?"**

The gap is not purely a data-access problem. It is a **decision-support problem**.

### Current experience

```text
Transaction happens
       ↓
Bank statement / app shows transaction
       ↓
User sees category or balance
       ↓
User must interpret what it means
       ↓
User may or may not change behavior
```

### Desired experience

```text
Transaction data
       ↓
MoneyFlow explains current state
       ↓
Highlights the most important change
       ↓
Suggests a simple action
       ↓
User acts
       ↓
MoneyFlow learns and improves the next check-in
```

## Problem hypothesis

We believe that users who receive a concise, explainable view of:

- current cash flow,
- upcoming recurring commitments,
- discretionary spending,
- progress toward a savings goal,

will be more likely to perform a meaningful financial-planning action than users who only receive passive dashboards.

## Why now?

The case study is positioned around a broader product shift from **information dashboards** toward **actionable personal decision support**.

The product should not compete on having the most charts. It should compete on reducing the cognitive work required to decide what to do.

## Product opportunity

**Opportunity:** Own the weekly/monthly financial check-in for young adults.

**Wedge:** "Know what is safe to spend and what action matters next."

## Success definition

MoneyFlow succeeds if users repeatedly:

1. understand their current position,
2. take a meaningful action,
3. return because the action helped them feel more in control.
